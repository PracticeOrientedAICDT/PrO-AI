Applications Open for BDFI’s Research Sandpit


Application deadline: 5pm, Monday 2 November

The Bristol Digital Futures Institute (BDFI) is offering three grants of up to £10,000 each to support pilot immersive projects with the potential to attract larger-scale future funding. University of Bristol academics and PhD researchers are invited to attend a sandpit event on 8 December 2026, 9am–5pm, to develop interdisciplinary projects addressing public challenges using BDFI’s immersive facilities, including the Reality Emulator and XR suite.

Projects may explore areas such as sustainability, training or public health. Funding can cover research, events, travel and staffing, and technical support may be available, so no prior technical expertise is required.

Find out more about the BDFI Research Sandpit and apply.



Have Your Say: World Café 2027


Expression of interest deadline: 10am, Wednesday 4 November

The World Café is a knowledge-sharing event that brings together PhD students from Science and Engineering CDTs to discuss common research challenges, exchange advice and share experiences across different stages of the PhD journey.

The next event is tentatively planned for early March 2027. Students are invited to complete a short expression of interest (EOI) survey to indicate whether they would like to attend and share their views on the event format, including the venue and whether it should be a half-day or full-day event.

Complete the World Café student EOI survey by 10am on Wednesday 4 November.

Find out more about previous events: World Café 2025 and World Café 2023.



Active Outreach Training


Would you like to deliver academic taster sessions for school or college-age students? The Widening Participation Team is offering Active Outreach training to help academic staff and postgraduate researchers develop the confidence and practical skills to plan and deliver engaging, inclusive outreach activities.

Find out more about upcoming Active Outreach sessions.



Robogals Bristol: Seeking Female Alumni Speakers


The University of Bristol’s Robogals Society is looking for female alumni to inspire the next generation of engineers and roboticists at a careers event on Monday 9 November, 6–7pm.

Alumni working in robotics, engineering, technology or related fields are invited to share their career journeys and insights with current students, helping to encourage more women to pursue careers in STEM. Previous involvement with Robogals is not required.

Interested in taking part? Email alumni-volunteers@bristol.ac.uk to find out more.



Enabling Spaces for Collective Intelligence


Wednesday 11 November 2026, 2 - 3pm, MVB 2.59 and SRB 3.315, or online via Microsoft Teams

The School Seminar Series launches with a talk by Seth Bullock, Head of the Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Research Group. The series will showcase research across the School, providing opportunities to exchange ideas and identify potential collaborations.

In this talk, Enabling Spaces for Collective Intelligence, Seth will explore how physical space shapes collective behaviour, from group-living animals and social networks to pandemics. He will discuss how spatial constraints can not only limit interactions but also help enable effective collective intelligence systems.



All Impact Club


17 November 2026, 2:00pm - 3:00pm, Online (Microsoft Teams)

The All Impact Club brings together colleagues interested in research impact to share knowledge, discuss current topics and learn from impact activities across the School and beyond. Each session will focus on a different topic.

For the first session, Ruzanna, School Research and Impact Director, will present a REF impact case study and explain the distinction between REF impact and broader forms of research influence and engagement.

If you have suggestions for future topics or would like to contribute to a session, please contact cs-staff-enquiries@bristol.ac.uk.



Bristol Doctoral College (BDC) Events and Opportunities


1.       Beyond the Thesis: Academic Funding Landscapes

3 November 2026, 10:30–11:30am, Online via Teams

Planning a future academic career? This online session offers a high-level introduction to the UK academic funding landscape, particularly for second- and third-year full-time PhD students.

Learn how to start researching funding opportunities in your subject area and build your understanding of academic funding to help demonstrate your awareness of, and commitment to, an academic career when applying for academic roles. Questions are welcome throughout via Teams chat.

Book your tickets here.

 

2.       ND PGR Speed Friending

5 November 2026, 4:00–5:30pm | Senate House (PGR Hub)

Looking to meet fellow postgraduate researchers in a relaxed and welcoming setting?

Join the Neurodivergent PGR Network for a structured social event open to all PGR students. You’ll meet in small groups, with tables shuffled throughout the session to help everyone connect. Conversation starters and fidget toys will be provided.

Booking is preferred but not required. you can still attend without it.

 

3.       Beyond the Thesis: Academic CVs

Tuesday 10 November | 10:30–11:30am | Online via Teams

Join the Bristol Doctoral College for an introductory session on building a strong academic CV, particularly suited to full-time PhD students in their second or third year.

The session will explore the purpose and structure of an academic CV, helping you identify relevant skills gained during your research, present them with appropriate context and evidence, and tailor your CV to specific academic roles. Questions are welcome throughout via Teams chat.

Book your tickets here.

For support with non-academic CVs, visit the Careers Service CV guidance. You can also contact the Careers Service to arrange an appointment or discuss your career options.

 

4.       Writing Precisely and Concisely

16 November 2026, 10:00–12:00, Senate House (PGR Hub)

A clear, concise abstract or summary can help communicate the value of your research. This workshop is open to postgraduate researchers at any stage of their degree and will explore techniques for writing more precise and engaging abstracts and summaries. You’ll also have the opportunity to draft your own abstract or receive peer feedback.

Book your tickets here.
